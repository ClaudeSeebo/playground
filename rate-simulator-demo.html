    The network, solving

    Five layers of artificial neurons, trained on the same rides by gradient descent. None of the layers bends the signal (no activation function), so the whole stack multiplies out to one formula: pickup + miles × rate + minutes × rate. What it learns is therefore a rate card, which you can check against the exact answer.

    How the card is fitted. Among all rate cards that bring in exactly the revenue actually collected, it picks the one whose prices sit closest to what each route charges now, with each route counted once per run (least squares). Every rate stays at or above its floor. The exact solver tries each combination of rates held at their floors and solves the rest directly; the network reaches the same card by gradient descent and stops when its own training signal settles, without looking at the exact answer.

    What it leaves out. Willingness to pay. A route priced above the card may be earning a premium riders happily pay; the card only shows where current prices depart from a consistent time-and-distance rule.
